# E-Commerce-growth-compass-Excel-SQL-Power-BI
This project shifted the executive team from reactive reporting to proactive reporting decision making, enabling them to make more informed decisions.

# Problem Statement & Project Goal
The Challenge: The company lacked a unified, real-time view of its e-commerce performance.
Key financial metrics such as Total Sales, Profit Margins, and Source Performance (which channels are most profitable) were scattered across different systems and raw datasets. 
This fragmentation prevented executive leadership from making fast, data-driven decisions regarding inventory, marketing budget allocation, and product strategy.

# The Goal: 
To consolidate all operational data into a single, clean source and develop an interactive Executive Dashboard in Power BI.
This dashboard, titled "E-Commerce Growth Compass", would provide instantaneous, actionable insights into profitability, customer spending habits, and high-value acquisition channels.
II. Data Preparation & Transformation (Excel)
Before loading the data into a structured environment, extensive cleaning and standardization were required in Excel. This critical phase ensured data quality, consistency, and proper data types for accurate analysis.

## Data Cleaning 
Initial pass to ensure data accuracy and remove redundant records. 
Duplicates were removed from rows across the dataset.
# Data Typing
Corrected non-numeric values that prevented calculation.
Removed spaces and string characters like currency symbols and texts  that were not needed.
# Data Standardization
Ensured dates were in a uniform, recognizable format for SQL and Power BI.
Changed the format of the order Date column to ensure consistency.
# Geographic Enrichment
Replaced abbreviated country names with full names for clearer geographic analysis.
Used VLOOKUP to match abbreviated country codes against a reference sheet containing full country names and replaced them in the main data.
# Business Logic
Calculated the core profitability metric at the transaction level.
Created the calculated Margin column by subtracting Cost from Price (Price-Cost).

# Analysis & Execution (SQL & Power BI)
This stage involved structuring the clean data in the SQL database, retrieving a master view, and building the necessary formulas in Power BI.
# SQL Data Structure and Retrieval
After preparing the raw tables, the cleaned data was imported into a SQL database. The primary action was to join the transactional data with customer, product, and review details into a single
and a comprehensive table (such as an order year table) suitable for Power BI consumption.

Key SQL Action: The SQL query used JOIN statements (including LEFT JOIN to preserve all transactions even if product or review data was missing) to create the final analytical data set.
SQL Explanation: The purpose of the SQL query was to combine separate tables (Orders, Customers, Products, Reviews) into one wide table. This makes filtering and cross-analysis in Power BI possible. 
It also performs calculated fields like the Calculated_Margin directly at the source.

# Power BI Data Modeling and Calculation
The consolidated SQL table was loaded into Power BI. Instead of using complex DAX for simple sums, Power BI's built-in aggregation was leveraged, and custom Time Intelligence was created:
1. Core Aggregation: The Total Sales measure was created using a simple SUM function (SUM('orders year'[Total])), and the Calculated Margin was used directly, relying on Power BI to sum the dollar values.
2. Time Series Analysis: To support the slicers and time-based charting, the Monthly and Yearly Sales Total was calculated by dragging the Total Sales measure onto visuals grouped by Month Name and Year,
  allowing for month-over-month (M-o-M) and year-over-year (Y-o-Y) comparison.

# Final Result & Positive Impact
The result is the Executive Dashboard picture, which delivers instant clarity on performance across four dimensions: Revenue, Profit, Source, and Customer Demographics.

## Key Insights & Features Explained by the Dashboard:
* Total Sales by Discount Bracket: This section (seen on the left) allows executives to quickly glance at the section dashboard by discount level (No Discount, Low Discount, High Discount),
 providing an immediate view of how discounting affects overall sales.
* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) (Top Cards):
* Low Rated Products Margin (2.62K): This is a critical risk metric. It shows the total dollar profit generated by products that customers rated poorly (e.g., 3 stars or less).
  A high number here signals a product quality issue despite profitability, directing immediate attention to those items.
* Sales Proceeds (4.49M): The overall total revenue generated, providing the high-level business health metric.
* Source Performance (Average Order Value & Discount Rate): The clustered column/bar chart compares the Average Order Value (AOV) against the Discount Rate by Acquisition Source
   (Referral, Paid, Direct, Social, Organic, Email).
   This visual answers the question: Which channel brings in the highest-spending customers (AOV) for the least amount of discounting?
* Customer Demographics (Average Spending by Age Group): The Donut Chart shows the percentage breakdown of spending by age group (e.g., Under 25, 25-40, Over 40).
  This directs marketing spend to the most financially valuable customer segments.
* Product Profitability (Total Profit by Category): This bar chart is the core profitability visual, showing the total dollar margin generated by product category
  (Electronics, Sports, Home & Kitchen, etc.). It guides inventory management and product focus.
* Geographic Performance (Average Order & Marketing Opt-In by Country): The line chart provides geographical context, showing how Average Order Size and Marketing Opt-In rates vary across different countries.

# Positive Business Impact:
This dashboard shifted the executive team from reactive reporting to proactive decision-making, enabling them to:
* Optimize Marketing Budget: By identifying high-AOV channels (e.g., 'Organic' or 'Referral') vs. high-discount channels (e.g., 'Social'), the team can shift spending toward profitable acquisition sources.
* Mitigate Product Risk: The Low Rated Products Margin card provides an early warning signal for product quality issues that could lead to future churn, allowing for timely investment in product improvement.
* Strategic Inventory Management: The Profit by Category chart guides decisions on which product lines to expand and which to reduce, maximizing dollar profit per square foot of warehouse space.
